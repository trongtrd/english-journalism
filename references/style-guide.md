# Editorial choices drawn from four Vietnam Weekly articles

Use these as conditional editing moves, not a house template. The corpus is small and purposively chosen. Section names and paragraph openings below identify where a move appears; they are evidence pointers, not prose to reproduce. The links are provenance only. This guide is sufficient for routine editing without reopening the articles.

## Source key

- **T1** — Michael Tatarski, [“The Textbook Shortage”](https://thevietnamweekly.substack.com/p/the-textbook-shortage), 18 September 2026; project file `archive/posts/2026/2026-09-18--the-textbook-shortage/article.md`.
- **T2** — Tatarski, [“A Vingroup Check-In”](https://thevietnamweekly.substack.com/p/a-vingroup-check-in), 23 September 2026; `archive/posts/2026/2026-09-23--a-vingroup-check-in/article.md`.

- **T4** — Tatarski, [“Small Businesses & The New Tax Rule”](https://thevietnamweekly.substack.com/p/small-businesses-and-the-new-tax), 18 June 2025; `archive/posts/2025/2025-06-18--small-businesses-and-the-new-tax/article.md`.

## Twelve choices

1. **Start with the material point, choosing scale or friction to suit the draft.** T1, “Textbooks in Short Supply,” moves from the school year to a national shortage and then parents visiting stores. T4 opens its tax discussion with the effective date and the businesses affected. **Repeated across T1/T4.** Use a lived consequence only when the draft supplies one; otherwise a measured change is a sound lead.

2. **Translate an administrative mechanism into what someone must do.** T4's opening tax paragraphs set out the invoice threshold, then describe the reported shift toward cash and the cost of adaptation. T1 places the authorities' restructuring explanation alongside the publisher's sole printing responsibility and a parent's quoted concern about that concentration; the sequence frames the access problem without independently proving what caused it. **Repeated across T1/T4.** Name the rule, threshold, or responsible institution before interpreting its impact; keep official explanations and quoted concerns attributed, and do not infer a causal link beyond the draft.

3. **Give the international reader one timely piece of context.** T1's HCMC textbook paragraph briefly explains the city's preceding administrative expansion. T2's first VinFast section identifies XanhSM/GSM as the company's taxi arm; T3's upgrade section distinguishes active from passive funds. **Repeated across T1/T2/T3.** Add a short explanation at first need, using known facts, rather than a background detour.

4. **Place a celebrated metric beside its measurement limit.** T2's VinFast delivery figures are followed by the unresolved split between individual buyers and ride-hailing fleets. T3's new trading-account count is followed by the caveat that accounts are not necessarily unique people. **Repeated across T2/T3.** Keep the number and attribution, then state only the limitation the draft supports.

5. **Separate announcement, projection, observation, and result.** T3's upgrade section distinguishes FTSE's potential inflow estimate, Vanguard's reported plans, actual foreign selling, and the long horizon for effects. T2 distinguishes VinFast's delivery goal from achieved deliveries and an expansion under consideration. **Repeated across T2/T3.** Preserve verbs such as estimates, plans, reportedly, and could; never promote a forecast into an outcome.

6. **Show skepticism through a specific evidentiary gap.** T4's “Counterfeit Concerns” weighs several reasons for shop closures and says a later month's data would be more informative. T2's VinFast discussion asks what fleet deliveries reveal about consumer demand. T3 recognizes the upgrade while presenting fund managers' objections. **Repeated across T2/T3/T4.** Pose the actual unanswered question and its consequence; avoid free-floating suspicion.

7. **Let the sentence of judgment follow the reporting.** In T1's influencer section, the author's concern about official direction follows the summit, database, and advertising-rule details. In T2's “The Land Piece,” the metro route's stated endpoint closes a run of real-estate and mangrove details. **Repeated across T1/T2.** A short, pointed conclusion is earned by the facts immediately before it and must not go further than the writer's existing claim.

8. **Acknowledge a policy's stated value while testing its execution.** T1's influencer section recognizes consumer protection before turning to state control; T4 describes the tax department's formalization goal alongside sellers' reported software and guidance problems. T3's market section recognizes the upgrade as meaningful even after limitations. **Repeated across T1/T3/T4.** Use this balance when the draft contains both sides; do not invent praise to make the criticism seem fair.

9. **Use first person as situated evidence, with its limits visible.** T2's red-light observation helps explain the saturation of XanhSM vehicles but does not stand in for sales data. T4 twice says the author has not personally seen the reported payment or closure patterns. **Repeated across T2/T4.** Retain a writer-supplied observation and its scope; never assign the writer a visit, sighting, or feeling they did not report.

10. **Break sections where the reporting changes question.** T2 moves from VinFast to property under “The Land Piece”; T3 separates the stock upgrade from trade talks; T4 marks the link to counterfeit enforcement. **Repeated across T2/T3/T4.** Use headings or short pivots for genuine topic changes, not to impose a fixed newsletter skeleton. T1's “Data Corner” and “Extra Links,” as well as greetings, subscription appeals, sign-offs, photo captions, and personal career notes, are format elements, not required style moves.

11. **Change sentence length when a finding needs room to land.** In T2's VinFast section, detailed sales and overseas context gives way to a brief observation about the difficulty of changing first impressions. In T3's market section, the account-count caveat is followed by a single-sentence scale marker. **Repeated across T2/T3.** A short sentence can mark the implication of prior evidence; it should not add a fresh claim or serve as a punchline after every dense paragraph.

12. **Use a light conversational pivot to return from a detour or qualify a conclusion.** T2 turns from the North Carolina dispute back to VinFast's stated delivery ambitions; T4 moves from competing explanations for closures to a bounded view of what lies ahead for small businesses. **Repeated across T2/T4.** Let the pivot clarify where the argument is going. The author's casual transitions are evidence of function, not stock phrases to insert, and they do not license invented humor or opinions.

## Voice boundary

Evidence here comes from Tatarski's own narrative choices and sequencing. Do not treat block quotations from officials, parents, journalists, or interviewees as his diction. Likewise, quoted slogans, headlines, linked article titles, and source paraphrases are claims by others. His occasional jokes and personal asides are visible in this sample, but they depend on a real authorial situation; they are not a license to invent intimacy or comedy. Write new sentences that perform the same editorial function, and preserve the draft writer's own stance.
