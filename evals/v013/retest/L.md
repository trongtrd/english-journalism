## English revision

# A Training Voucher Is Only Part of the Decision

Province L's training voucher pilot has two headline figures: by the end of September, the provincial department reported 180 vouchers issued and 120 workers who had completed a course. Those are separate totals, not a completion rate. Its report has no linked record showing which voucher holders completed, and neither figure counts firms. The report also contains no productivity measure.

The six-month pilot began in April. Registered small manufacturers could recover half the fee for an approved course, up to 2 million dong per worker. Firms had to pay the full fee first, then request reimbursement after a worker completed the course and submitted the required attendance certificate. Officials said the scheme was meant to help firms upgrade workers' skills. The department did not report how long reimbursement requests took to process, so we cannot say whether delays discouraged participation.

The fee, however, is only one part of whether a firm can send a worker to class. In August, an independent local business association spoke with 14 participating employers. The association recruited them from its members, so their answers are not a representative survey of small manufacturers. Nine said weekday classes made it difficult to release workers; five did not report that problem. The association did not ask how many production hours were lost, whether shifts could be swapped or whether firms hired temporary cover. Its interviews identify a scheduling difficulty among some participants, not its cost. They also tell us nothing directly about eligible firms that did not join.

Two approved providers offered evening classes from August. The department combined evening and daytime enrollments, so the report does not show how many joined evening classes or whether those classes eased the scheduling problem.

I would keep the pilot for a second, limited round at the same reimbursement level, while holding off on a province-wide rollout. In that round, I would collect linked enrollment and completion records and ask participating and nonparticipating firms about scheduling and upfront payment. I would also compare the evening and daytime options before recommending broader expansion. Training deserves support, but the question now is whether firms can use this voucher, not merely how many were issued. Nor can the present report show that the pilot improved productivity.
