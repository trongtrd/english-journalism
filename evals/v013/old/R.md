## English revision

# The case for one service point needs better evidence

District C's administrative table shows average licensing time falling from 18 calendar days in the first quarter to 15 in the second: a three-day decrease. That is an improvement in the reported average, but it does not show what caused it. The table gives no case counts, mix of applications or definition of when the processing clock starts and stops.

An undated presentation says 80% of firms are satisfied. Without the survey question, number of respondents or method, that figure cannot tell us how broadly the view is shared. Nor can the available material establish that the new one-stop service has removed the burden on small businesses, or that firms outside the district centre have benefited.

I still favour expanding the shared-service approach across the province. A single service point seems easier to navigate than several separate offices. That is a policy preference, however, rather than a finding that the model has saved time for every firm. As it expands, the province should publish comparable processing data and a documented account of firms' experience.
