# Fictional scenario — editing exercise

## English revision

# A Training Voucher Is Only Part of the Decision

Province L's six-month training voucher pilot has produced two clear totals: by the end of September, 180 vouchers had been issued and 120 workers had completed a course. It has not produced a completion rate. The provincial department reported those figures separately, without a linked record showing which voucher holders completed. Neither figure counts firms, and the report includes no measure of productivity.

That distinction matters before a program designed to upgrade workers' skills is rolled out more widely. Launched in April, the pilot lets registered small manufacturers recover half the fee for an approved course, capped at 2 million dong per worker. Firms pay the full fee upfront and can request reimbursement only after a worker completes the course and submits an attendance certificate. The department has not published how long processing takes, so there is no basis to say whether reimbursement delays discouraged participation.

The course fee is only part of the decision. In August, an independent local business association spoke with 14 participating employers recruited from its members. Nine said releasing workers for weekday classes was difficult; five did not report that difficulty. This convenience sample is not representative of small manufacturers. Nor did the association ask about lost production hours, shift swaps or temporary cover. The answers point to a scheduling problem for some participants, without establishing its cost or explaining why eligible firms stayed out.

Two approved providers began offering evening classes in August. But the department combined their enrollments with those of daytime providers, so the report cannot show whether the evening option eased the problem.

I support keeping the pilot for a second, limited round at the current reimbursement level. I would collect linked enrollment and completion records, ask both participating and nonparticipating firms about scheduling and upfront payment, and compare evening and daytime options. Training support is worthwhile, and another round could show whether firms can use it. The current report does not justify a province-wide expansion or a claim that the pilot has raised productivity.

## Giải thích

- Before: `We cannot use the two numbers to calculate a completion rate.` After: `It has not produced a completion rate.` Đưa giới hạn của hai số liệu lên ngay sau kết quả được công bố; không lấy 120 chia cho 180 khi thiếu hồ sơ liên kết.
- Before: `This was reimbursement after completion, rather than a discount when enrolling.` After: `Firms pay the full fee upfront and can request reimbursement only after a worker completes the course and submits an attendance certificate.` Diễn giải cơ chế thành các bước doanh nghiệp phải thực hiện, nhưng không suy đoán thời gian hoàn tiền.
- Before: `Nine said releasing workers for weekday classes was difficult.` After: `Nine said releasing workers for weekday classes was difficult; five did not report that difficulty.` Giữ cả hai nhóm trả lời và giới hạn của mẫu thuận tiện, tránh biến phỏng vấn thành kết luận cho mọi doanh nghiệp.
- Before: `It is therefore too early to conclude that evening classes solved the scheduling problem.` After: `the report cannot show whether the evening option eased the problem.` Gắn nhận định với thiếu sót cụ thể của báo cáo: số ghi danh lớp tối bị gộp với lớp ban ngày.
