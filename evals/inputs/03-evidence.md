# Pilot 03 — unsupported and conflicting claims

Synthetic scenario. The district, firms and figures are fictional. The draft intentionally contains evidence problems. Do not research real-world facts or invent sources. Treat the provided notes as the only evidence packet.

## User request

Edit my English draft for an international policy newsletter, using only my draft and the notes below. Give the revised text and 3–5 explanations in Vietnamese. Make any substantive correction or unresolved evidence problem visible to me. Do not silently change my policy preference.

## Draft

The new one-stop service in District C has cut licensing times in half. The average processing time went from 18 days in the first quarter to 15 days in the second. This proves that moving officials into one building has removed the burden on small businesses. A local business association says 80% of firms are satisfied, so there is no reason to delay rolling the model out across the province. I favour expansion because a shared service point is easier to navigate than several separate offices. A manufacturer told me that its manager used to spend every Monday travelling between offices, but now everything is finished in a morning. The change is good news for all firms, especially those outside the district centre.

## Author's notes

- The administrative table reports average processing times of 18 and 15 calendar days. It does not give case counts, application types or a definition of when the processing clock starts or stops.
- The 80% figure was copied from an undated presentation. The survey question, number of respondents and method are missing. There is no link available.
- I did not conduct the manufacturer interview. That sentence is an illustrative placeholder written during drafting, not testimony or a quotation that can be published as fact.
- I favour expanding the shared-service approach. This is my policy preference, not an established finding about its effects. The final draft should preserve the preference while making the evidence limits clear.
